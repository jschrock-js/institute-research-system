Final Report: Colorado Fentanyl Penalties Ballot Measure

Overview:
In November 2025, a coalition of Republican state lawmakers, law enforcement officials, and families announced the collection of over 200,000 signatures to qualify a ballot measure in Colorado. This initiative aims to significantly increase penalties related to fentanyl offenses within the state (Colorado ballot measure for tougher fentanyl penalties moves forward, https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2025/11/20/colorado-ballot-measure-for-tougher-fentanyl-penalties-moves-forward/?utm_source=openai).

Key Provisions of the Measure:
- The measure proposes classifying possession of any amount of fentanyl as a felony offense, regardless of the quantity involved (Fentanyl penalties ballot measure signature collection, https://www.cpr.org/2025/11/20/fentanyl-penalties-ballot-measure-signature/?utm_source=openai).

- Distribution or sale of fentanyl could result in mandatory prison sentences ranging from 8 to 32 years under the proposed law (Fentanyl penalties ballot measure signature collection, https://www.cpr.org/2025/11/20/fentanyl-penalties-ballot-measure-signature/?utm_source=openai).

- The initiative includes an alternative for low-level fentanyl possession offenders, allowing courts to order substance use treatment instead of incarceration (Fentanyl penalties ballot measure signature collection, https://www.cpr.org/2025/11/20/fentanyl-penalties-ballot-measure-signature/?utm_source=openai).

Fiscal Impact:
Legislative Council Staff projected that implementing the measure would initially increase state expenditures by approximately $856,770 during the first fiscal year, rising to nearly $4.9 million in the second fiscal year (Fiscal impact statement for the fentanyl penalties ballot measure, https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/initiatives/2026%252385FIS_00.pdf?utm_source=openai). These estimates depend on assumptions regarding enforcement actions and incarceration rates, and actual costs may vary.

Support and Opposition:
Supporters:
- The conservative nonprofit Advance Colorado endorses the measure, emphasizing the urgency of combating fentanyl overdoses and asserting that current laws are insufficient (Colorado ballot measure for tougher fentanyl penalties moves forward, https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2025/11/20/colorado-ballot-measure-for-tougher-fentanyl-penalties-moves-forward/?utm_source=openai).

Opponents:
- Democratic lawmakers, public health experts, and other critics caution that the measure may disproportionately criminalize individuals suffering from addiction without effectively reducing overdose deaths (Fentanyl penalties ballot measure signature collection, https://www.cpr.org/2025/11/20/fentanyl-penalties-ballot-measure-signature/?utm_source=openai).

- There are concerns that harsher criminal penalties could exacerbate racial disparities in incarceration, potentially impacting communities of color more heavily; however, specific data on racial impacts related to this measure are not yet available (Fentanyl penalties ballot measure signature collection, https://www.cpr.org/2025/11/20/fentanyl-penalties-ballot-measure-signature/?utm_source=openai).

- Experts such as Dr. Josh Barocas have noted that evidence from previous similar laws does not support the effectiveness of increased criminal penalties in reducing overdose mortality (GOP-backed ballot measure would make any fentanyl possession a felony in Colorado, https://www.kunc.org/news/2025-11-20/gop-backed-ballot-measure-would-make-any-fentanyl-possession-a-felony-in-colorado?utm_source=openai).

- Critics further argue that the measure places greater emphasis on punishment rather than on prevention and treatment programs, potentially missing opportunities to address addiction comprehensively (Fentanyl penalties ballot measure signature collection, https://www.cpr.org/2025/11/20/fentanyl-penalties-ballot-measure-signature/?utm_source=openai).

Limitations and Uncertainty:
- The exact legal language of the measure is pending final drafting; thus, legislative amendments or judicial rulings could alter its provisions before or after enactment.

- Fiscal projections rely on estimates that may not fully capture real-world enforcement or incarceration patterns following implementation.

- Data on racial disparities specifically attributable to this measure are not yet available; concerns are inferred from broader criminal justice trends.

- While expert opinions indicate that harsher penalties have not historically reduced overdose deaths, the potential impact of this particular initiative remains uncertain.

- Accessibility and effectiveness of court-ordered substance use treatment alternatives depend on program implementation details, which remain unclear.

Conclusion:
The Colorado fentanyl penalties ballot measure represents a bipartisan coalition effort to address the fentanyl crisis by imposing stricter criminal penalties, including felony charges for any fentanyl possession and mandatory prison sentences for distribution. It offers alternatives focused on treatment for low-level offenders and is projected to increase state expenditures substantially. The measure garners support for its intent to curb overdose deaths but faces criticism regarding its potential to increase incarceration, exacerbate racial disparities, and overlook prevention and treatment strategies. Continued evaluation and monitoring will be crucial to understanding the measure's effects if passed.
