**Final Report on Colorado Initiative 85: Fentanyl Possession Penalties**

---

### 1. Bottom Line

Initiative 85, slated for the 2026 Colorado ballot, proposes making possession of any amount of fentanyl a felony offense, with mandatory prison sentences and enhanced penalties for intent to distribute. The initiative also includes provisions for court-ordered treatment alternatives for some low-level offenders. While supporters argue this measure is necessary to combat rising fentanyl overdose deaths and deter dealers, opponents raise concerns about increased incarceration and insufficient focus on addiction treatment. Fiscal analyses predict a significant rise in state expenditures and personnel. However, notable uncertainties remain regarding the measure’s effectiveness at reducing overdose deaths, its impact on incarceration rates, and potential unintended consequences such as discouraging individuals from seeking help.

---

### 2. Key Findings

- **Felony Possession:** Initiative 85 proposes that possession of any quantity of fentanyl be classified as a felony with mandatory prison terms. (Source: Colorado Public Radio)

- **Enhanced Sentences for Distribution:** Those convicted of possession with intent to sell or distribute could face 8 to 32 years imprisonment under the measure. (Source: Colorado Public Radio)

- **Treatment Alternatives:** The initiative allows courts to order substance use treatment for certain low-level offenders as an alternative to incarceration. (Source: Colorado Public Radio)

- **Fiscal Impact:** The measure is expected to increase state expenditures substantially—from approximately $857,000 annually to over $4.9 million within two years—and result in additional state personnel. (Source: Colorado Legislative Council Staff Fiscal Impact Statement)

- **Political Support:** The conservative advocacy group Advance Colorado supports the initiative and successfully gathered over 200,000 signatures to place it on the ballot. (Source: Colorado Politics)

- **Supporters’ Rationale:** Supporters, including Republican lawmakers and law enforcement officials, cite increases in fentanyl overdose deaths as justification for tougher penalties. (Source: Colorado Public Radio)

- **Opposition Arguments:** Opponents—mainly progressive Democrats and substance use advocates—warn the measure may lead to higher incarceration without addressing addiction’s root causes, advocating for treatment-focused approaches instead. (Source: Colorado Public Radio)

- **Expert Opinion on Effectiveness of Penalties:** Some experts, such as Dr. Josh Barocas from the University of Colorado, note that harsher criminal penalties have not demonstrably reduced overdose deaths, suggesting treatment may be more effective. This insight is based on expert opinion rather than comprehensive empirical data. (Source: KUNC)

- **Potential Negative Consequences:** Critics express concern that criminalization could deter fentanyl users from seeking help due to fear of legal repercussions, though empirical evidence on this point is lacking. (Source: Colorado Public Radio)

- **Ballot Qualification:** The initiative has qualified for the 2026 general election ballot, pending no significant legal challenges. (Source: Colorado Newsline)

### 4. Key Evidence Gaps

- **Effectiveness on Overdose Deaths:** There is limited empirical data currently available to confirm whether harsher fentanyl possession penalties reduce overdose fatalities. Expert opinions suggest minimal impact, but robust, long-term studies or real-world outcome data are needed.

- **Impact on Incarceration Rates:** While opponents anticipate increased incarceration, actual effects after implementation are unknown. Data on how many offenders will receive treatment versus prison time under the initiative’s provisions is lacking.

- **Behavioral Consequences:** Claims that criminalizing fentanyl possession may discourage individuals from seeking addiction help or treatment are anticipatory and not yet supported by empirical studies.

- **Scope of Treatment Alternatives:** The precise criteria for qualifying for court-ordered treatment versus incarceration remain subject to legal interpretation and potential modification; clarity on this is limited.

- **Fiscal Projections Under Variable Conditions:** Fiscal impact estimates might change with implementation strategies, caseload variation, and evolving drug trends.

- **Long-Term Social and Community Effects:** Data on broader social impacts, such as community safety, public health outcomes, and recidivism, are not available.

---

### 5. Policy Tradeoffs

- **Supporters’ Position:** The initiative promises stronger legal deterrents against fentanyl possession and trafficking, aiming to address skyrocketing overdose deaths and increase public safety. It proposes some avenues for treatment to reduce incarceration for low-level offenders.

- **Opponents’ Position:** Critics worry the measure’s punitive focus could exacerbate mass incarceration, strain state budgets, and fail to tackle substance use disorders’ underlying causes. They advocate for expanded access to addiction treatment and prevention services.

- **Fiscal Considerations:** The initiative is projected to significantly increase state spending and require new personnel, raising questions about resource allocation balance between enforcement and treatment programs.

- **Uncertainty and External Factors:** Legal challenges, political shifts, and evolving drug patterns may influence how the initiative is implemented and its ultimate impact.
