# Final Report on Colorado Initiative 85: Increasing Penalties for Fentanyl-Related Crimes

---

## 1. Bottom Line

Initiative 85 proposes to increase criminal penalties for fentanyl-related offenses in Colorado by elevating felony classifications for distribution, manufacturing, dispensing, sale, or possession of fentanyl and certain synthetic opioids. The initiative, qualifying for the 2026 ballot with substantial public support, is projected to increase state revenues through higher fines but will also significantly raise state expenditures due to increased judicial, public defender, and incarceration costs. There is no direct empirical evidence that these increased penalties will reduce fentanyl-related overdose deaths or drug misuse. Furthermore, the initiative does not include provisions to expand access to substance use disorder treatment programs, potentially limiting its effectiveness in addressing the fentanyl crisis comprehensively. 

---

## 2. Evidence-Supported Findings

- **Provisions of Initiative 85**: The measure explicitly seeks to elevate felony classifications for fentanyl and certain synthetic opioid offenses, including distribution, manufacture, and possession. Certain cases also include mandatory treatment provisions. (High certainty; official Colorado Secretary of State ballot description)

- **Ballot Qualification and Public Support**: The initiative was placed on the 2026 ballot after proponents submitted over 180,000 petition signatures, exceeding the required threshold, indicating strong public backing to date. (High certainty; official state election sources)

- **Fiscal Impact**: Legislative Council Staff estimates project that the initiative will lead to net increases in state expenditures ranging from approximately $860,000 to $4.9 million over two years, primarily due to increased costs in courts, public defense, and corrections. Concurrent revenue increases from higher fines and fees are projected to be $20,000 to $123,000 but do not offset expenditures fully. (High certainty; official fiscal impact statement)

- **Absence of Treatment Expansion**: The initiative does not include provisions to expand access to treatment programs for substance use disorders. This is a factual policy gap that may limit the initiative’s capacity to reduce overdose deaths or recidivism. (High certainty; analyzed from initiative text)

---

## 3. Evidence Quality and Source Support

- **Supporter Claims (Deterrence and Reduced Overdose Deaths)**: Advocates assert that stricter penalties will deter fentanyl distribution and reduce overdose fatalities. These claims are based on advocacy perspectives without empirical data. (Level 1: Weak/speculative evidence; low certainty)

- **Opponent Claims (Over-incarceration and Disproportionate Impact)**: Opponents express concern that the initiative could increase over-incarceration and disproportionately affect individuals with addiction, especially youth or those unaware of fentanyl presence. These are advocacy-based concerns lacking post-implementation empirical validation. (Level 1: Weak/speculative evidence; low certainty)

- **Research on Incarceration and Drug Misuse**: Indirect evidence from reputable secondary sources suggests that higher incarceration rates do not necessarily reduce drug misuse or drug-related problems. This context is informed by research cited secondarily and does not directly evaluate Initiative 85. (Level 2: Theory-informed, limited; medium certainty with caution due to secondary sourcing)

- **Outcome Data Gap**: There is no direct evidence that the increased penalties will reduce fentanyl-related overdose deaths in Colorado since the initiative is not yet enacted, and no longitudinal evaluation exists. (Level 1: Weak/speculative; very low certainty)

---

## 4. Exploratory and Tentative Insights (Hypothesis-Generating)

- Increasing criminal penalties without expanding treatment may increase prison populations without meaningfully addressing the demand or supply factors underlying fentanyl misuse.
  
- Mandatory treatment provisions in the initiative might face implementation challenges if existing treatment capacity is insufficient, potentially limiting rehabilitation benefits.

- Heightened penalties for individuals unaware of fentanyl presence could undermine trust in criminal justice systems and public health collaboration.

- Political and public momentum behind the initiative may overshadow consideration of alternative, health-focused approaches to the fentanyl crisis.

---

## 5. Key Evidence Gaps

- Empirical evidence on the initiative’s real-world impact on fentanyl-related overdose deaths, drug misuse rates, and recidivism is unavailable and will only become assessable post-enactment.

- Independent judicial impact studies are lacking to confirm or refute claims regarding sentencing severity and potential disproportionate effects, especially among young or first-time offenders.

- Original research directly linking incarceration rates to reductions in drug misuse specific to fentanyl-related offenses and Colorado’s context remains absent.

---

## 6. Relevant Systems Effects and Unintended Consequences

- Elevated felony classifications may lengthen prison terms even for minor infractions, potentially increasing long-term incarceration rates and exacerbating social disparities.

- Increased demand on judicial and correctional systems could strain resources, leading to delays or reduced quality in court processing and rehabilitative services.

- Greater fiscal burdens on the state with uncertain offsetting revenue gains may reduce funding availability for other public safety or health initiatives.

- Over-penalization risks may contribute to community disruption and impose barriers to reintegration, thereby possibly perpetuating cycles of addiction and crime.

---

## 7. Tradeoffs

- Initiative 85 offers a tough-on-crime legal approach appealing to public demand for action against fentanyl distribution, likely yielding increased sentence severity as a deterrent intent.

- However, the absence of expanded treatment programs means it addresses supply reduction efforts without simultaneously providing demand-side interventions, possibly limiting comprehensive crisis mitigation.

- Higher penalties may increase incarceration costs substantially, imposing continued financial and social costs on state systems and affected communities.

- Policymakers face the challenge of balancing enforcement priorities with public health strategies to effectively address opioid-related harms.

---

## 8. Limitations

- Fiscal impact estimates are projections with inherent uncertainty and may vary with future case volumes, legal interpretations, and economic conditions.

- Advocacy-based evidence from both supporters and opponents lacks empirical validation and should be interpreted as perspectives rather than proven outcomes.

- Secondary citation of research (e.g., Pew Trusts) limits certainty about incarceration impact claims due to lack of direct source examination.

- The initiative’s actual effects will depend significantly on implementation fidelity, judicial discretion, and availability of treatment resources beyond the measure’s text.

# Appendix E: Systems + Multidisciplinary Analysis

1. Exploratory Insights (Tentative & Hypothesis-Generating):

- Enhancing penalties without expanding treatment access may shift more fentanyl users and low-level distributors into the prison system, potentially heightening incarceration rates without substantially reducing demand or availability of fentanyl.
- The measure’s mandatory treatment provisions, while limited, might create a bottleneck if treatment capacity is insufficient, unintentionally limiting the intended benefits of rehabilitation.
- Public support driven by the perceived urgency of fentanyl-related deaths might overshadow careful consideration of long-term social costs, including community disruption from increased incarceration.
- Over-penalization of individuals unaware of fentanyl presence may foster distrust in the justice system, discouraging cooperation with law enforcement and aggravating public health challenges.

2. Evidence-Supported Insights:

- Initiative 85 clearly aims to increase criminal penalties by elevating felony levels for fentanyl-related offenses, confirmed by official state resources.
- The measure has strong public backing, evident by the collection of significantly more than the required petition signatures, demonstrating political momentum.
- Fiscal projections indicate net increased state costs due to judicial, public defender, and incarceration expenses, outweighing revenue gains from fines.
- There is no empirical evidence that harsher penalties will effectively deter fentanyl crimes or reduce overdose deaths; existing research suggests higher incarceration does not necessarily curb drug misuse.
- The initiative does not expand treatment access for substance use disorders, limiting its comprehensive effectiveness in addressing the crisis.

3. Second- and Third-Order Effects (Plausible & Relevant):

- Increased incarceration could exacerbate post-release challenges, including employment barriers and housing instability, potentially reinforcing cycles of addiction and crime.
- Elevated felony classifications may affect sentencing guidelines and plea bargaining dynamics, possibly resulting in longer prison terms even for minor infractions.
- Fiscal strain on the criminal justice system might reduce funding or capacity for other public safety or social services, indirectly impacting community well-being.
- Political framing of the initiative as a "tough-on-crime" measure may influence future drug policy debates, potentially sidelining harm reduction or public health-focused strategies.

4. Unintended Consequences and Implementation Risks:

- Risk of disproportionately penalizing low-level offenders and individuals with unintentional fentanyl exposure, which could worsen racial and socioeconomic disparities in incarceration.
- Judicial and corrections systems may face resource strains, leading to delays or reduced quality in legal proceedings and rehabilitation efforts.
- Without concurrent investments in treatment infrastructure, mandatory treatment provisions may not effectively reduce recidivism or overdose risk.
- The balance between increased revenue from fines and burgeoning expenditures is uncertain; if costs substantially exceed projections, state budgets may be negatively impacted.

5. Non-Obvious Implications for Decision-Makers:

- Decision-makers should consider the opportunity cost of allocating resources predominantly to enforcement and incarceration rather than expanding treatment and harm reduction services, which might yield better long-term outcomes.
- The political success of the initiative may constrain future reforms aimed at more holistic addiction treatment strategies due to hardened positions.
