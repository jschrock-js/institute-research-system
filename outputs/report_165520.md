# Final Report: Proposed Colorado Ballot Measure on Fentanyl Penalties

## Introduction

In November 2025, a significant initiative was introduced in Colorado aimed at addressing the state’s escalating fentanyl crisis through stricter legal penalties. This report synthesizes key claims, supporting evidence, and ongoing developments related to the proposed ballot measure that seeks to increase penalties for fentanyl-related offenses. The initiative exemplifies the broader debate on balancing punitive measures and treatment-focused approaches to substance misuse.

## Overview of the Proposed Measure

The proposed ballot measure, backed by the conservative political nonprofit Advance Colorado, intends to substantially increase penalties for fentanyl offenses across the state. If approved, the initiative would:

- Make possession of **any amount** of fentanyl a felony.
- Impose mandatory prison sentences ranging from **eight to 32 years** for individuals convicted of possessing fentanyl with intent to sell or distribute.
- Allow for **court-ordered treatment** alternatives instead of incarceration for some low-level offenders.

This proposal was publicly announced by Republican state lawmakers, law enforcement officials, and family members of overdose victims at a November 2025 press conference outside Denver’s Ralph Carr Judicial Center.

## Public Support and Ballot Qualification

The initiative garnered significant public backing, with **approximately 200,000 signatures** submitted to the Secretary of State’s office, surpassing the required threshold of around 125,000 valid signatures needed to qualify for the ballot. This level of support demonstrates strong voter interest in legislative solutions targeting fentanyl-related crimes.

The Secretary of State’s office is responsible for reviewing and verifying these signatures. Following certification, the initiative is slated to appear on the **2026 statewide ballot**, providing Colorado voters with the opportunity to approve or reject the measure.

## Context: Fentanyl Crisis and Legislative Background

Fentanyl overdoses have surged dramatically in Colorado, with fatal incidents **more than quadrupling from 2019 to 2022**, according to the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment. In response, the Colorado General Assembly enacted the **Fentanyl Accountability and Prevention Act in 2022**, which:

- Classified possession of 1 to 4 grams of fentanyl as a **Level 4 drug felony**.
- Maintained possession of less than 1 gram as a **misdemeanor**.

The new ballot initiative seeks to build upon this framework by criminalizing possession of *any* fentanyl amount as a felony and increasing penalties for intent to sell or distribute.

## Debate and Perspectives

The measure has sparked debate between proponents and opponents regarding the most effective strategies to combat the fentanyl epidemic:

- **Supporters**, including many Republicans and law enforcement advocates, emphasize the necessity of harsher penalties to deter fentanyl distribution and protect public health.
  
- **Opponents**, primarily Democrats and progressive groups, argue that increasing criminal penalties disproportionately punishes individuals struggling with addiction rather than targeting drug dealers. They advocate for greater investment in treatment and harm reduction programs. For example, Democratic State Representative Javier Mabrey expressed concern that "[t]his is going to result in people who are sick [...] spending time in prison when they should have access to treatment," highlighting the view that substance use disorder is a public health crisis requiring therapeutic rather than punitive responses.

## Current Status and Outlook

As of April 2026, the measure has completed initial legislative processes, including review and comment by the Legislative Council Staff and the Office of Legislative Legal Services. The text of the initiative has been publicly presented according to constitutional and statutory requirements.

The measure is scheduled to appear on the **2026 Colorado ballot**, where voters will decide its fate. Its outcome will reflect Colorado’s ongoing efforts to find a balance between safeguarding public safety through law enforcement and addressing addiction as a health issue.

## Conclusion

Colorado's proposed ballot measure on fentanyl penalties represents a pivotal moment in the state's response to the opioid crisis. Backed by strong public support and aiming to impose stringent penalties on fentanyl possession and distribution, the initiative embodies one approach within a broader, complex public policy debate. The 2026 voter decision will significantly shape Colorado’s legal and health strategies relating to fentanyl in the coming years.
