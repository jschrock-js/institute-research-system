
# Final Report

**Final Report: Analysis of Colorado Initiative 85 (2026 Fentanyl Penalties Ballot Measure)**

---

### 1. Bottom Line

Colorado Initiative 85 proposes significant increases in felony penalties for fentanyl-related offenses and introduces mandatory court-ordered treatment for certain low-level possession felonies. Official analyses project modest revenue increases but substantial expenditures in judicial, public defender, and incarceration costs. While supporters argue tougher penalties will combat the fentanyl crisis, available empirical evidence supporting overdose reduction from increased penalties is limited and uncertain. The initiative raises important equity and fiscal concerns, with potential unintended consequences requiring careful monitoring and further research.

---

### 2. Evidence-Supported Findings

- **Legal and Policy Provisions:** Initiative 85 elevates felony classifications for fentanyl-related crimes—including distribution, manufacturing, dispensing, sale, and possession—and narrows sentencing exemptions, especially for offenses resulting in death. It mandates court-ordered treatment for individuals convicted of specified low-level fentanyl possession felonies. *(Strong evidence; Colorado Secretary of State official text)*

- **Fiscal Impact Projections:** According to the Colorado Legislative Council Staff, the initiative is expected to increase state revenues modestly via higher fines and fees from felony drug offenses (estimated $19,516 in FY 2026-27 escalating to $71,142 in FY 2027-28). Expenditures are anticipated to rise substantially, with costs related to judicial processes, public defenders, and prison operations growing from approximately $857,000 in FY 2026-27 to nearly $4.9 million in FY 2027-28, and an estimated $44.6 million increase in prison costs over five years. *(Strong evidence; official fiscal impact statements)*

- **Supporter Claims on Overdose Reduction:** Proponents assert that increased penalties will deter fentanyl-related crimes and reduce overdose deaths, referencing rising fatalities as justification. However, these claims are primarily advocacy-based without direct empirical validation specific to the initiative. *(Limited evidence; stakeholder argument)*

- **Concerns About Effectiveness and Treatment Access:** Independent research not directly tied to Initiative 85 suggests that punitive measures, including increased incarceration, do not necessarily correlate with reductions in overdose mortality. Studies indicate that fear of legal penalties may discourage individuals with substance use disorders from seeking treatment, potentially undermining court-ordered treatment benefits. These findings are based on general research with indirect applicability to the initiative. *(Limited theory-informed evidence)*

- **Equity Considerations:** Broader research highlights disproportionate impacts on marginalized communities in drug law enforcement outcomes. While no direct data links Initiative 85 to such disparities in Colorado, equity concerns are plausible given national trends on heavier policing and incarceration of communities of color for drug offenses. *(Limited theory-informed evidence)*

---

### 3. Evidence Quality and Source Support

- Core legal provisions and fiscal projections derive from authoritative, official sources with high certainty, suitable for describing policy design and anticipated budget impacts.

- Public health outcome claims (e.g., overdose reduction) by supporters lack rigorous empirical support— these remain speculative and should be presented as stakeholder perspectives.

- Evidence on punitive measures' limited effectiveness in reducing overdose deaths and on deterrence of treatment seeking is theory-informed but indirect and not linked specifically to Initiative 85, warranting cautious interpretation.

- Equity impact concerns arise from reputable general research but are unconfirmed regarding this initiative’s enforcement or demographic effects in Colorado.

---

### 4. Exploratory or Tentative Insights (Hypothesis-Generating)

- **Behavioral Impacts:** Increased felony penalties and mandatory treatment might deter some fentanyl-related criminal activity but could also push drug use and low-level distribution underground, limiting treatment engagement and possibly heightening health risks.

- **Treatment Mandates:** Court-ordered treatment may help some individuals but might reduce treatment efficacy if imposed without addressing voluntary motivation or providing adequate support.

- **Fiscal Tradeoffs:** Increased incarceration and judicial costs under Initiative 85 may divert resources from preventive and harm reduction programs, potentially undermining broader public health efforts.

- **Policing and Social Inequities:** Heightened enforcement might deepen systemic racial and socioeconomic disparities unless accompanied by equitable monitoring and safeguards.

---

### 5. Key Evidence Gaps

- Absence of robust, Colorado-specific empirical data linking increased fentanyl penalties with reductions in overdose deaths or improved public health outcomes.

- Lack of direct studies on implementation effectiveness of mandatory court-ordered treatment provisions under Initiative 85.

- Insufficient data on demographic enforcement patterns and the initiative’s differential impact on marginalized populations.

- Uncertainty regarding long-term judicial and incarceration cost trajectories and related budgetary tradeoffs.

---

### 6. Relevant Systems Effects / Unintended Consequences

- **Incarceration-Driven Social Effects:** Expanded prison populations may destabilize families and communities, potentially increasing long-term substance use risks and social fragmentation.

- **Judicial System Strain:** Increased caseloads and mandatory treatment enforcement may overload courts and public defenders, potentially affecting case processing times and outcomes.

- **Drug Market Dynamics:** Elevated penalties could incentivize traffickers to adopt covert, riskier distribution strategies, complicating law enforcement and public health responses.

- **Voluntary Treatment Deterrence:** The threat of felony charges linked to possession might discourage voluntary engagement with health and harm reduction services.

---

### 7. Tradeoffs

- **Public Safety vs. Public Health:** Balancing deterrence through tougher penalties with fostering access to voluntary treatment and harm reduction is critical but challenging.

- **Short-Term Costs vs. Long-Term Benefits:** The initiative’s higher immediate fiscal costs could theoretically reduce future fentanyl harms, but evidence does not confirm this causal pathway.

- **Equity and Justice:** Without targeted safeguards and data-informed adjustments, Initiative 85 risks exacerbating disparities rooted in current drug law enforcement regimes.

---

### 8. Limitations

- The report relies on projections and general research rather than localized outcome evaluations.

- Some cited studies are not directly linked or specific to the initiative’s context, limiting causal inference.

- Stakeholder and media claims reflect opinions, not robust scientific consensus.

# Appendix E: Systems + Multidisciplinary Analysis

Certainly. Here's an organized, multidisciplinary analysis of Colorado’s 2026 Fentanyl Penalties Ballot Measure (Initiative 85):

---

### 1. Exploratory Insights (Tentative, Hypothesis-Generating)

- **Behavioral Responses to Increased Penalties:** Heightened felony classifications and mandatory treatment might deter some from fentanyl-related crimes but could also drive users and low-level dealers further underground, reducing their likelihood of seeking help or complying with treatment. This could increase risky behaviors or unmonitored drug use.

- **Treatment Mandates as a Double-Edged Sword:** Court-ordered treatment may benefit some individuals by connecting them to services; however, mandatory participation without addressing underlying social support or voluntary motivation could reduce long-term treatment success and increase recidivism.

- **Policing and Social Inequality Feedback:** If the initiative leads to intensified policing of fentanyl offenses, especially in marginalized communities, systemic racial and socioeconomic disparities in incarceration and treatment access might deepen, perpetuating cycles of disadvantage.

- **Fiscal Tradeoffs and Resource Allocation:** Rising incarceration and judicial costs could crowd out funding for preventive public health programs, harm reduction initiatives, or community-based substance use services that might more effectively reduce fentanyl harms.

---

### 2. Evidence-Supported Insights

- **Legal and Fiscal Dimensions:** Initiative 85 will unequivocally increase penalties for fentanyl-related crimes and introduce mandatory court-ordered treatment for certain possession offenses (Colorado Secretary of State).

- **Projected State Budget Impact:** According to Colorado Legislative Council Staff, the initiative will raise state revenues modestly via increased fines but will substantially increase expenditures on incarceration, judicial processes, and public defenders—costs potentially reaching tens of millions over five years.

- **Unproven Public Health Impact:** Claims that tougher penalties will meaningfully reduce fentanyl overdoses or deaths lack solid empirical backing; key studies suggest that increased incarceration is not correlated with lower overdose rates (American Journal of Public Health, limited evidence).

- **Potential Treatment Access Disincentives:** There's moderate evidence from substance abuse research that fear of legal penalties can discourage treatment-seeking behavior, which may counteract the benefits of mandatory treatment programs linked to felony convictions.

---

### 3. Second- and Third-Order Effects (Plausible and Relevant)

- **Incarceration-Driven Community Effects:** Beyond budget strain, increased prison populations may destabilize families and communities, potentially elevating the long-term risk of substance use, unemployment, and social disintegration in affected neighborhoods.

- **Judicial System Bottlenecks:** The rise in prosecutions and mandatory treatment enforcement could burden courts and public defenders, potentially causing delays and lowering the overall quality of judicial outcomes.

- **Opportunity Costs in Public Health:** Funds allocated to expanded incarceration under Initiative 85 might reduce support for harm reduction strategies (e.g., naloxone distribution, safe consumption spaces), which are shown in some contexts to reduce overdose deaths more effectively.

### 4. Unintended Consequences and Implementation Risks

- **Disproportionate Impact on Marginalized Groups:** Without safeguards, mandatory treatment and elevated penalties may exacerbate racial and socioeconomic disparities, as existing policing biases could lead to uneven enforcement.

- **Deterring Voluntary Treatment:** The threat of felony charges and mandatory treatment may stigmatize users, reducing voluntary engagement with health services and harm reduction programs.

- **Fiscal Overreach Risk:** If incarceration costs escalate beyond projections, balanced state budgeting may become harder, pressuring lawmakers to cut other critical services.
