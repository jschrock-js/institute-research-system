**Final Report: Colorado’s 2026 Ballot Initiative on Stricter Fentanyl Penalties**

In November 2025, a coalition comprising Republican state lawmakers, law enforcement officials, and families affected by fentanyl overdoses announced a significant milestone: the successful collection of over 200,000 signatures to place a ballot measure—Initiative 85—on Colorado’s 2026 ballot. This initiative reflects a growing bipartisan concern over the fentanyl crisis that has severely impacted the state and the nation.

**Key Provisions of Initiative 85**

Initiative 85 seeks to impose notably stricter penalties for the possession and distribution of fentanyl. Under the measure, any possession of fentanyl, regardless of quantity, would be classified as a felony offense. Individuals convicted of manufacturing, dispensing, or distributing fentanyl would face mandatory prison sentences ranging from eight to 32 years. However, the initiative also includes provisions offering court-ordered substance use treatment for certain low-level offenders, specifically those caught with up to one gram of fentanyl.

**Fiscal Impact and Implementation**

According to the Legislative Council Staff’s fiscal impact statement, implementing Initiative 85 would result in a modest increase in state revenue—estimated at approximately $19,516 in the first year and $71,142 by the second year. However, these revenue gains would be overshadowed by substantial increases in state expenditures, which could rise from about $856,770 initially to nearly $4.9 million. Additionally, the initiative would necessitate a growth in personnel, expanding from an estimated 6.0 to 11.7 full-time equivalent staff members, primarily to manage increased judicial and correctional system demands.

**Stakeholder Perspectives**

Supporters of Initiative 85, including Republican officials, law enforcement, and the conservative nonprofit Advance Colorado, argue the measure is essential to curtail the alarming rise in fentanyl-related overdose deaths. They believe harsher criminal penalties will serve as a deterrent to drug dealers and enhance community safety. District Attorney George Brauchler underscored this urgency, describing fentanyl as "a poison like nothing I’ve seen in my 30-plus years in the criminal justice system."

Conversely, opponents—mainly progressive Democrats and substance use advocates—express concern that the punitive approach criminalizes addiction rather than addressing its root causes. They emphasize that similar laws in other states have not yielded positive outcomes and advocate for treatment-focused and public health strategies instead. Dr. Josh Barocas of the University of Colorado Anschutz School of Medicine highlighted the ineffectiveness of comparable laws elsewhere, underscoring the need for alternate approaches.

**Next Steps**

Having qualified for the 2026 ballot, Initiative 85 will be decided by Colorado voters. The measure encapsulates a complex debate over tackling the fentanyl crisis: balancing stringent law enforcement and incarceration measures with calls for comprehensive treatment and prevention strategies. The upcoming electoral decision presents an opportunity for Colorado’s electorate to shape the state’s policy direction on a critical public health and safety issue.
